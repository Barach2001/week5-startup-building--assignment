# Kakuma Connect — Startup Pitch Deck

**Founder:** Barach Aduot  
**Stage:** Concept / first draft  
**Initial focus:** Kakuma, Kenya

> A mobile-friendly platform connecting residents to local services, small businesses, opportunities and community information.

## Slide 1 — Title
**Kakuma Connect**  
A trusted digital doorway to local services, opportunities and community information.

## Slide 2 — The Problem
- Service information is scattered across word of mouth, posters and separate online groups.
- Residents may struggle to discover nearby businesses, available services and legitimate opportunities.
- Small businesses and community providers can find it difficult to reach the people who need them.
- Information can be outdated, incomplete or hard to verify.

**Core problem to validate:** people need one easier way to find useful, current local information.

## Slide 3 — The Solution
- A searchable directory of local services and small businesses.
- A curated opportunities board for jobs, training, scholarships and volunteering.
- Community notices and practical updates in one place.
- Clear contact details, location information and ways to report incorrect listings.

Kakuma Connect helps residents find information and helps local providers become easier to discover.

## Slide 4 — How It Works
1. Open Kakuma Connect on a phone or lightweight web page.
2. Search or browse by category: services, businesses, opportunities or notices.
3. Review the listing, date, location and contact details.
4. Contact the provider directly; report information that looks wrong or outdated.

The first version focuses on discovery and connection—not payments or complex transactions.

## Slide 5 — Who We Serve
- **Primary users:** residents looking for local services and verified opportunities.
- **Local providers:** small businesses, service providers, community groups and training organisations.
- **Secondary users:** NGOs, education partners and employers sharing relevant opportunities.
- **Initial geography:** Kakuma; expand to nearby host-community areas only after learning from a pilot.

## Slide 6 — Market Opportunity
- **Beachhead:** residents and local providers in Kakuma who need better information discovery.
- **Expansion path:** nearby host-community areas, then other underserved communities where the model fits.
- Potential demand comes from repeated needs: finding services, comparing options and discovering opportunities.
- No market-size figure is claimed yet; population, smartphone access and provider counts must be researched.

**Research next:** estimate reachable users and active providers using credible local data and field interviews.

## Slide 7 — Business Model
- Free access for residents to browse essential listings and public opportunities.
- Affordable promoted listings for businesses and service providers (clearly labelled as sponsored).
- Paid organisation tools or service packages for partners that need enhanced listing management.
- Potential sponsorships for opportunity campaigns or community information projects.
- Pricing will be tested with providers before launch; no revenue is claimed today.

**Trust principle:** paid placement never means an opportunity is verified.

## Slide 8 — Go-to-Market
- Recruit a small group of community organisations and service providers as pilot partners.
- Collect and verify an initial set of useful listings before inviting residents.
- Promote through community groups, youth networks, local organisations and in-person demonstrations.
- Use feedback, referrals and repeat usage to improve the product.
- Track listing accuracy, successful contacts and returning users.

Launch in one focused area, learn, improve and then expand.

## Slide 9 — Competitive Landscape
- Current alternatives include word of mouth, posters, social-media groups and separate organisation websites.
- These channels can be useful, but information may be fragmented, difficult to search or quickly outdated.
- Differentiation: local focus, categories built around community needs, dated listings and reporting tools.
- Kakuma Connect will complement trusted community channels rather than replace them.

Competitive advantage must be earned through accuracy, useful coverage and community trust.

## Slide 10 — Validation & Milestones
- **Discovery:** interview residents, providers and community organisations about current search habits.
- **Prototype:** test a simple mobile-friendly directory with a small set of manually checked listings.
- **Pilot:** measure searches, listing views, contacts made, repeat visits and correction reports.
- **Decision gate:** improve or change the concept based on real usage and user feedback.
- **Current status:** concept-stage; no paying customers, active users or traction figures are being claimed.

Success for the first pilot means proving that people use it and providers find it valuable.

## Slide 11 — Team & Execution
- **Founder:** Barach Aduot — concept development and community-focused product direction.
- Early support needed: local research volunteers, community reviewers and a developer/design mentor.
- Partner roles: help identify trusted sources, validate listings and share the pilot with users.
- Build approach: start with a low-cost web prototype, then prioritise features from user feedback.

The team will grow around demonstrated needs and practical partner commitments.

## Slide 12 — The Ask & Next Steps
Kakuma Connect is seeking:
- Pilot partners who can help validate needs and share accurate local information.
- Mentorship in product design, software development, safeguarding and sustainable business models.
- Access to a small pilot group of residents and local providers for interviews and usability testing.

**Next 90 days:** research needs → prototype directory → run a small pilot → review evidence and refine.
